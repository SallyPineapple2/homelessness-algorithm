Overview

VI-SPDAT and Gemini Housing Priority

Gemini run

Pilot question

Given the same 100 people, how does Gemini's own judgment of housing priority compare with the VI‑SPDAT's?

Approach
VI‑SPDAT is a comparison benchmark, not ground truth. Both approaches place each person in High, Medium, or Low priority, so they are compared category to category.
Caution
A difference between groups does not by itself show bias: groups can differ in circumstances. Controlling for circumstances is the next step.

Design

Population. 100 distinct synthetic adults. 32 are the original study's base profiles, adapted to one person each; 68 are new. Every case summary states each VI‑SPDAT item outright, yes or no, so neither approach has to guess.

Demographics. The original study's 2 genders, 5 races, and 5 cities. Age comes with each person's story. Gender, race, and city were assigned by a seeded draw stratified on VI‑SPDAT score: every race and every city has 20 people (10 women, 10 men), and every race × city pair has 2 women and 2 men, so each group gets a similar mix of need. Pregnant profiles are women.

VI‑SPDAT. Version 2.0 for single adults: 17 items, one point each. Scores 0–3 recommend no housing intervention (Low), 4–7 Rapid Re‑Housing (Medium), and 8–17 Permanent Supportive Housing (High).

Gemini. Gemini's own judgment, not a VI‑SPDAT calculation. Each person is one independent API request containing only the instructions and that person's case summary, so earlier cases cannot influence later ones. No system instruction; default settings. Gemini never saw the VI‑SPDAT questions, items, rules, or scores, or the titles and IDs used on this page.
