Algorithmic Bias in Homeless Vulnerability Assessment

VI‑SPDAT vs. AI‑Based Scoring in the Coordinated Entry System (CES)

Research Question

When analyzing the same synthetic client data, how does an AI‑based (LLM) vulnerability assessment perform — in terms of accuracy and demographic bias — compared with the VI‑SPDAT algorithm currently used to prioritize unhoused individuals for shelter and services?

Hypothesis: the AI‑based model will outperform VI‑SPDAT in accuracy against true vulnerability, but will still show a residual skew against people of color.

Data Source

Primary source: the published VI‑SPDAT interview methodology and scoring rubric (OrgCode Consulting Inc., 2015), accessed via the Georgia Department of Community Affairs — 16 vulnerability indicators across 4 domains, scored 0–17. This project builds a synthetic population (32 base profiles × 2 genders × 5 HUD race/ethnicity categories, each tested with full and partial disclosure) and scores it with both VI‑SPDAT and an LLM standing in for an ML‑based CES tool.

Supporting sources: C4 Innovations, “CES Racial Equity Analysis of Assessment Data”; the Coordinated Entry System Triage Tool Research and Refinement report; and published documentation from CoCs piloting ML‑based CES replacements.
